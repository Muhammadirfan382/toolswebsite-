## Count every character

Many places limit text by characters rather than words: social media posts, bios, SMS messages, product titles and search-engine snippets. This character counter updates as you type and shows how your text compares with the most common limits, so you can trim it before posting.

## What it shows

- **Characters**, including spaces, shown large at the top.
- **Characters without spaces.**
- **Words**, **lines** and **sentences**.

Each emoji or accented letter counts as one character, the way a person sees it. This matches how most people think about length, although some platforms count differently behind the scenes.

## Platform limits at a glance

The "Platform limits" panel compares your text with common limits and turns each bar **amber** when you are close and **red** when you are over, with the exact number of characters left or over:

- **X (Twitter) post:** 280 characters. X counts some characters, such as emoji and links, differently, so use this as a guide.
- **Page title for SEO:** about 60 characters. Search engines cut titles by width, so this is a guideline rather than a hard limit.
- **Meta description for SEO:** about 155 characters, a common guideline for search result snippets.
- **Instagram caption:** 2,200 characters.
- **SMS text message:** see below.

## SMS: why 160 sometimes becomes 70

A single SMS can hold **160 characters** when it uses only the standard SMS character set (basic Latin letters, digits and common punctuation). If the message contains an emoji, a curly quote, or letters from scripts such as Urdu, Arabic or Hindi, the phone switches to Unicode, and one SMS holds only **70 characters**.

Longer messages are sent in parts of 153 or 67 characters. The counter tells you which encoding your message needs, how many parts it will use, and which character forced the switch to Unicode, so you can replace it if you want to stay in a single message. A few symbols, such as € and square brackets, count as two characters in the standard set.

## Where character limits matter

- **Social media:** posts, bios, captions and profile names all have maximum lengths.
- **Search results:** page titles and meta descriptions that are too long get cut off with an ellipsis.
- **Online shops and marketplaces:** product titles and short descriptions are often limited.
- **Forms and applications:** some fields accept only a set number of characters, including spaces.
- **Text messages:** longer SMS messages are split into several parts, which can cost more on some plans.

## Characters with or without spaces?

Most platforms, including social networks and SMS, count spaces as characters. Some academic and publishing guidelines give limits "without spaces". The counter shows both, so you can use whichever your instructions ask for. Line breaks count as characters too.

## Tips for fitting a limit

- **Cut filler words** like "very", "really" and "in order to".
- **Use numerals** instead of spelled-out numbers.
- **Front-load the key message**, since long titles and snippets are often cut off at the end.
- **Avoid unnecessary emoji in SMS**, which can halve the space available.
