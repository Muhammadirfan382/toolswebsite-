## Find an exact age in seconds

Knowing someone's age in whole years is easy. Knowing it exactly, in years, months and days, takes more care: months have different lengths, leap years add a day, and birthdays on the last day of a month need special handling. This age calculator does that counting for you and shows how it got the answer.

## What the calculator shows

- **Age in years, months and days**, such as 24 years, 3 months, 12 days.
- **Totals**: age expressed as total months, total weeks (with remaining days), total days and total hours.
- **Day of the week you were born**, for example Tuesday.
- **Your next birthday**: the date, the day of the week, and a countdown in days, plus the age you will turn.

Results appear as soon as both dates are filled in. Press "Copy result" to paste a summary into a form or a message.

## The formula behind the result

The calculator uses calendar arithmetic rather than dividing days by 365:

1. Count the whole months from the date of birth to the target date. A month is complete when the same day number is reached in a later month.
2. Divide those months by 12: the quotient is the **years**, the remainder is the **months**.
3. Count the **days** left between the last completed month and the target date.

For example, from 15 May 1990 to 22 September 2024 there are 412 complete months (34 years and 4 months), plus 7 days from 15 September to 22 September. So the age is 34 years, 4 months and 7 days.

When a birth date falls on a day that a later month does not have, such as the 31st, the month is treated as complete on that month's last day. The same rule means that a **29 February birthday is counted as 28 February** in years that are not leap years.

## When an exact age matters

- **School and university admissions** often set a minimum or maximum age on a cut-off date.
- **Job and exam applications** ask for age on the closing date of the advertisement.
- **Sports and competitions** group people by age on a set day.
- **Retirement, pensions and insurance** calculations often depend on age at a specific date.
- **Personal milestones**, such as counting down to a birthday or celebrating 10,000 days old.

For any of these, set "Age at this date" to the date stated in the rules, not today. Always follow the official instructions if they define age differently.

## Checking an age limit

Application rules are often written as "at least 18 years old on 1 January" or "under 30 on the closing date". To check one:

1. Enter your date of birth.
2. Set "Age at this date" to the date named in the rules.
3. Compare the years, months and days with the limit. If the rules say "under 30", someone aged exactly 30 years and 0 days does not qualify.
