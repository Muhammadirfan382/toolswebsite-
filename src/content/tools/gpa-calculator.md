## Your grade point average, calculated properly

A GPA (grade point average) turns letter grades into a single number. Because courses carry different numbers of credit hours, a four-credit course affects your GPA more than a two-credit one. This calculator does the weighted arithmetic for you and shows exactly how it reached the result.

## The formula

**GPA = Σ(Grade points × Credit hours) ÷ Σ Credit hours**

Worked example:

| Course | Credits | Grade | Points | Points × credits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematics | 3 | A | 4.0 | 12.0 |
| Physics | 4 | B+ | 3.3 | 13.2 |
| Art | 2 | C | 2.0 | 4.0 |
| **Total** | **9** | | | **29.2** |

GPA = 29.2 ÷ 9 = **3.24**.

Notice that a simple average of 4.0, 3.3 and 2.0 would be 3.10. The weighted GPA is higher because the courses with better grades carried more credits.

## Grade scales differ

The calculator starts with a common 4.0 letter scale used by many schools, especially in the United States. Many institutions differ: some give A+ a value above 4.0, some have no minus grades, and some use entirely different point values. Grading scales vary by school, so check your institution's official scale.

Open **"Edit grade points"** to change the value of any grade. Your changes are applied immediately and kept in the page link, so a shared link shows the same scale. Press "Reset to default" to go back to the standard values.

## Tips for an accurate GPA

- **Use official credit hours** from your course catalog or transcript.
- **Leave out courses that do not count**, such as pass/fail or audited courses, if your school excludes them.
- **Rows that are only partly filled in are skipped**, and the calculator tells you which course is missing a grade or credits when you press "Calculate".
- **Check repeated courses.** Schools treat retakes differently; follow your school's rule.

## Planning with "what if" grades

The calculator is also useful before results come out. Enter the courses you are taking this term with the grades you expect, and see what GPA they would produce. Change one grade at a time to see which course has the most effect: a course with more credit hours moves your GPA further than a smaller one.

## GPA and percentages are not interchangeable

Letter grades are usually awarded from percentage bands, but those bands differ between schools and even between courses. A GPA cannot be turned back into an exact percentage without your school's own table. If an application asks for a percentage, use the figure or conversion method from your official transcript.
