## Five calculators in one

Percentages come up everywhere: exam results, shopping discounts, pay rises, tax, statistics in the news. Most questions fall into a handful of types, so this percentage calculator has a separate tab for each, with its own clearly labeled boxes. Every answer comes with the formula and the working, so you can check it or copy the steps into homework.

## The formulas

### 1. What is X% of Y?

**Result = (X ÷ 100) × Y**

Example: 20% of 350 = 0.2 × 350 = 70. Use this for tips, commission, or a share of a total.

### 2. X is what percent of Y?

**Percentage = (X ÷ Y) × 100**

Example: 42 out of 60 = 0.7 × 100 = 70%. Use this to see what share one amount is of another.

### 3. Percentage increase or decrease

**Change % = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100**

Example: a price going from 80 to 100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% increase. A positive answer is an increase and a negative answer is a decrease. The starting value cannot be zero, because a change from zero has no percentage.

### 4. Marks percentage

**Percentage = (Obtained marks ÷ Total marks) × 100**

Example: 387 out of 450 = 86%. The calculator checks that obtained marks are not more than the total.

### 5. Add or subtract a percentage

**Add: Result = Y + (X% of Y). Subtract: Result = Y − (X% of Y).**

Example: adding 17% tax to 120 gives 120 + 20.4 = 140.4. Taking 30% off 80 gives 80 − 24 = 56.

## Everyday examples

- **Shopping:** a jacket costs 80 and is 25% off. Use "Add / subtract %": 80 − 20 = 60.
- **Tips:** a 12% tip on a bill of 45. Use "X% of Y": 45 × 0.12 = 5.4.
- **Pay rise:** a salary goes from 2,000 to 2,150 a month. Use "% change": (2,150 − 2,000) ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 7.5%.
- **Exam results:** 68 out of 80 marks. Use "Marks %": 68 ÷ 80 × 100 = 85%.
- **Budgeting:** rent of 700 from an income of 2,500. Use "X is what % of Y": 700 ÷ 2,500 × 100 = 28%.
- **Prices with tax:** a price of 50 before 8% sales tax. Use "Add / subtract %" with Add: 50 + 4 = 54.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Percentage points vs percent.** If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it rose by 1 percentage point, which is a 25% increase.
- **Reversing a discount.** Taking 20% off and then adding 20% back does not return to the original price: 100 becomes 80, and 80 plus 20% is 96.
- **Using the wrong base.** Percentage change is always measured against the starting value.
- **Marks and weightings.** If a course weights exams differently, a simple marks percentage may not match your official grade. Check your institution's rules.
